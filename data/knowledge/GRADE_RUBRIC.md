# Grade rubric — Institutional memory

**Owner:** CoS (closes grades) · Riley may assist with evidence  
**SoT SOP:** `docs/writers/INSTITUTIONAL_MEMORY.md`

Grades are **sacred**. Prefer leaving a card `open` over inventing a grade.

## Grades

| Grade   | Definition                                                                                         | Lesson expectation                                      |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| `right` | Outcome clearly supports the claim **as filed** (same as-of frame)                                 | What signal / process to keep                           |
| `wrong` | Outcome clearly contradicts the claim as filed                                                     | What broke (assumption, SoT, process) — no blame theater |
| `mixed` | Material parts right and wrong; or right direction / wrong magnitude that matters for the product | Spell the split explicitly                              |
| `void`  | Cannot grade honestly: event cancelled, market never posted, claim malformed, scope error          | Why voided; do not pretend it was right/wrong           |

## Evidence rules

1. Cite a concrete outcome or authoritative post-event source (result, draft order, final market settlement, official release).
2. Tie evidence to the **as-of** claim — do not grade a restated claim after the board moved unless the card was restamped.
3. House / model / market views on the card are context for the lesson — the grade is on the **claim** text.
4. No evidence → leave `open` or set `void` with reason. **Do not invent.**

## Who may close a grade

- **CoS** closes / approves.
- **Riley** may draft the grade + evidence table; CoS still owns the ledger close unless CoS delegates in writing on the card.
- **Writers** file claims; they do not self-close grades without CoS.

## Anti-patterns

- Inventing 2024–2026 (or any) grades to “seed” the ledger
- Grading EXAMPLE cards as real history
- Softening `wrong` into `mixed` without a real split
- Using chat memory as the only record of a lesson
