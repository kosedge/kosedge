# Arizona Cardinals 2026 Season Preview

**By Morgan Hale** · Kos Edge Analytics · NFC West desk  
**Angle:** A two-win model gap meets a basement total and an unfinished depth chart  
**Date:** August 26, 2026  
**Market fact-check:** August 26, 2026 · DraftKings via RotoWire · Editor Riley Nash  
**Model SoT:** nfl-preseason-sim-2026-20260822T013711Z (N=100000) expected_wins  
**Market (DK / RotoWire, Aug 2026 fact-check):** Win total **4.5** (Over +125 / Under -150)  
**Sources (beat desk):** Josh Weinfuss (ESPN); Bo Brack (PHNX Sports); Darren Urban (AZCardinals.com)

Arizona has the largest positive Model-to-market gap in the NFC West and the least stable case for turning that gap into a bet. The Kos Edge simulation puts the Cardinals at **6.55 expected wins**. DraftKings asks whether they can reach five. Ordinarily, a 2.05-win difference would start an Over discussion. Here it starts a model-audit discussion.

The market is not merely hanging a low number. It is charging **-150** on the Under while offering **+125** on the Over. That is a direct warning that roster quality, quarterback transition and divisional strength are being priced more harshly than the simulation. A basement total also behaves differently from a mid-board total: one extra close-game win matters more, but the downside has already been compressed. The correct response to that disagreement is **Pass**, not a louder Over.

## Brissett raises the floor, not the ceiling

The quarterback question is settled enough for a season preview. General manager Monti Ossenfort described Jacoby Brissett as the starter in August, and the Cardinals adjusted Brissett’s contract after his role changed from backup to projected QB1. Rookie Carson Beck remains a developmental piece and has been managing sore ribs. Gardner Minshew II supplies veteran depth.

That hierarchy gives Mike LaFleur a functional starting point. Brissett can keep the offense on schedule, distribute to Marvin Harrison Jr. and Trey McBride, and reduce the number of weeks lost to reckless quarterback play. None of that makes Arizona explosive by default. Brissett missed offseason work during his contract situation and has been learning a new system on a compressed calendar. The passing game must create efficiency through structure because the roster cannot assume it will win repeatedly through individual mismatch advantages.

The distinction matters at 4.5. A competent veteran quarterback can move a bad team from noncompetitive to live in the fourth quarter. He cannot guarantee that those games convert into five wins, especially when the other three teams in the division all carry win totals of 10.5 or higher.

## The late-camp roster is still moving

Arizona’s Aug. 24 transactions clarified both sides of the roster. The club activated edge rusher Josh Sweat from the physically unable to perform list, giving its top pass rusher almost three weeks to prepare for the Sept. 13 opener at the Chargers. That is the most important positive camp development. A defense with limited margin needs pressure without sending extra rushers, and Sweat is the clearest player capable of supplying it.

The backfield moved the other way. Arizona waived Trey Benson with an injury designation after knee trouble interrupted another camp. Rookie Jeremiyah Love was still out of practice while progressing from an ankle sprain. That combination concentrates uncertainty behind the starting offense at the exact position LaFleur would prefer to use to protect Brissett. It also makes preseason backfield production a poor guide to the regular-season run plan.

There are additional availability constraints. Tight end Tip Reiman was not expected to be ready for Week 1 while recovering from an ankle injury. Cornerback Garrett Williams came off PUP on Aug. 20 after an Achilles tear, but activation is only the first step toward a full defensive workload. The Cardinals also lost guard Chase Bisontis and rookie tackle Kaleb Proctor to injured reserve earlier in camp. Those are depth losses, not headline injuries, but depth is where a five-win team’s path usually breaks.

The net camp read is mixed: Sweat’s return raises the defensive ceiling, while the backfield and line reduce the offense’s error tolerance. There is no clean research case that confirms the Model’s 6.55 as a betting fair strong enough to ignore the market’s price.

## What 6.55 means against 4.5

The Kos Edge number is an expected-win output from 100,000 simulations, not a promise that seven wins is the most likely exact finish. It says the team-level inputs generate more competitive outcomes than the public board implies. Brissett’s floor, Harrison’s receiving gravity and the possibility of improved pass rush all contribute to that path.

The opposing case is structural. The Under bettor needs Arizona to finish with four wins or fewer, and the market charges heavily for that outcome. Six games come against the Rams, Seahawks and 49ers, whose posted totals are 11.5, 10.5 and 10.5. Arizona can improve on offense and still go 1-5 in the division. It can also play several one-score games and lose enough of them to stay below five.

That is why neither price is attractive. Over bettors are being offered plus money and a large model gap, but they must trust an unsettled roster to convert close games. Under bettors are laying **-150** into the most compressed part of the win curve. The market and Model are describing materially different teams. Until early regular-season efficiency explains which description is wrong, there is no threshold-approved ticket.

## Schedule and game-shape pressure

The opener at the Chargers is a useful stress test. Arizona’s offensive line must handle a road environment while Brissett operates a new system, and Sweat’s conditioning will be tested immediately. More broadly, the division supplies six games against opponents priced as contenders. That schedule does not make five wins impossible; it reduces the number of forgiving weeks.

Arizona’s best path is narrow but visible: protect the football, let Harrison and McBride win defined targets, generate pressure with four, and turn late possessions into field-position games. Its failure path is also visible: early-down inefficiency exposes the line, the thin backfield cannot stabilize the offense, and a rebuilding defense spends too many snaps protecting short fields.

That profile may produce useful weekly underdog prices. It does not require a preseason win-total position.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total 4.5:** **Pass.** Model fair is 6.55, but the 2.05-win disagreement with a heavily juiced Under is a conflict to resolve, not permission to force an Over.
- **Weekly sides:** Arizona can become attractive as a home underdog when the offensive line is intact and Sweat’s snap share is normal. Avoid assuming every large divisional spread is automatically value.
- **Team totals:** Brissett’s first-down efficiency and the backfield rotation should determine the early-season view. Preseason depth production is not enough.
- **Player markets:** Wait for stable playing time in the backfield and confirmed target distribution in LaFleur’s offense. No season prop position is warranted from camp allocation alone.
- **Division futures:** Pass. A low win total does not make a long division price efficient when all three rivals are posted at 10.5 wins or higher.
