Dallas has enough offense to win ten games. That is not the same as saying ten should be the betting expectation.

DraftKings opened the Cowboys at **8.5** in February and now asks them to clear **9.5**, with the Under taxed to **-140**. The Kos Edge Model lands much lower at **7.10 expected wins**. That 2.40-win disagreement is impossible to hide, but it is not an automatic Under ticket. The market has aggressively priced an intact offense and a redesigned defense; the Model remains skeptical that the conversion from 7-9-1 to double-digit winner happens this quickly. When two credible views are that far apart, the disciplined answer is **Pass**, not false precision.

## The offense is not the argument

Dallas finished 2025 second in total offense and seventh in scoring at **27.7 points per game**. Dak Prescott threw for 4,552 yards. CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens each cleared 1,000 receiving yards, while Javonte Williams gave Brian Schottenheimer a 1,200-yard runner. The Cowboys can create explosives without abandoning the run, and all four central pieces return.

That continuity explains why the board moved. It also explains why the Under costs so much. Bettors are not being offered a stale 8.5 against a broken offense; they are being asked to lay a premium against one of the league's best skill groups.

The leverage point is protection. Dallas has high-end interior talent around Tyler Smith, Cooper Beebe, and Tyler Booker, but camp did not erase the tackle questions. The Cowboys' Aug. 25 roster review noted that Terence Steele did not have his best summer and that no challenger clearly displaced him. Nate Thomas improved against Arizona, yet his strongest case remains as the swing tackle. Tyler Guyton's ability to hold the left side matters because moving pieces around the line is how a clean offense becomes a third-and-long offense.

The encouraging depth note came in the 34-13 preseason win at Arizona: the reserve line helped Dallas run for 175 yards and did not allow a sack of Joe Milton III. That is useful evidence for the floor. It is not evidence that the starting tackles can be ignored against Philadelphia, Baltimore, Houston, Green Bay, San Francisco, Seattle, and the Rams.

## The defense has to earn the move

The 2025 split was severe. Dallas allowed **30.1 points per game**, worst in the league, while the offense scored enough to keep the team in games it had no business controlling. Christian Parker now inherits a unit that could feature seven new starters and is shifting to a 3-4 base.

The front has recognizable answers. Quinnen Williams and Kenny Clark give the interior real weight. Rashan Gary is the primary edge addition, and Von Miller joined a rotation that also includes Donovan Ezeiruaku and first-rounder Malachi Lawrence. Caleb Downs and Jalen Thompson remake the safety room, while Cobie Durant adds competition outside.

That is a legitimate overhaul. It is also a communication test. New personnel, new front mechanics, and a young safety handling NFL route distributions can create a better December defense without producing a clean September. Parker even used a simulated headset failure in camp to test DeMarvion Overshown's command; Overshown got the call right. Good process matters, but Dallas must repeat it when opponents use tempo and motion.

The late-camp health sheet is manageable rather than pristine. Jonathan Bullard has dealt with groin and abdomen issues, though the club expressed optimism. Julius Wood was evaluated for a concussion after the Arizona game. The larger concern is not one current absence. It is whether an overhauled group can turn last year's league-worst scoring defense into even an average unit before the schedule starts taking bites.

## The schedule charges interest

The opening sequence is hostile to a defense still learning its language. Dallas starts at the Giants on Sunday night, hosts Washington, then travels to Rio de Janeiro to face Baltimore. From there come a road game at Houston, a short-week home date with Tampa Bay, a trip to Green Bay, and a Monday night game at Philadelphia.

The back half does not hand back much. San Francisco visits in Week 10. Philadelphia comes to Arlington on Thanksgiving. Dallas then travels to Seattle, takes a late Week 14 bye, and returns with a road game against the Rams. Jacksonville and a second Commanders game remain on the sheet.

That sequence creates more than generic strength-of-schedule pressure. It creates installation pressure, international travel, short rest, and a very late reset week. A ten-win Over needs Dallas to win most of the games it should win while stealing multiple games from the contender tier. The offense makes that possible. The defense has not yet made it probable.

## Market math

The Model distribution is wide: its 10th-to-90th percentile band runs from **three to 11 wins**. The mean is not claiming Dallas cannot reach ten. It is saying the route requires a favorable mix of defensive improvement, tackle health, and close-game conversion.

At the same time, laying **-140** on Under 9.5 asks the bettor to pay a large toll for the Model's skepticism. The number already moved a full win from the opener. That combination—large projection conflict, expensive Under, and a roster with genuine offensive ceiling—is why this is a Pass.

The cleaner early-season approach is to evaluate the defense in pieces. Track early-down success allowed, pressure created without blitzing, explosive passes conceded, and how quickly Downs and the corners pass off motion. If those indicators settle near league average, the market's ten-win case will have support. If Dallas still needs 30 points every week, the schedule will expose it without help from a preseason narrative.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total 9.5:** **Pass.** The Model's **7.10** fair is materially below the market, but **-140** on the Under and a high-end offense make the Model-market conflict too large to treat as a routine edge.
- **Line movement:** The move from an **8.5** opener to **9.5** is meaningful. Do not handicap the stale number.
- **Early-season trigger:** Reassess only after the new defense shows it can prevent explosives and win third down against starting quarterbacks.
- **Schedule watch:** The Brazil-Houston-Tampa Bay-Green Bay-Philadelphia run is the pressure point; travel and short rest amplify a new defensive install.
- **Team props and player production:** Prescott, Lamb, Pickens, and Williams can produce strong individual seasons even if Dallas finishes below ten wins. Do not force team-win skepticism into unrelated player Unders.
