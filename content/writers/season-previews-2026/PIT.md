**Sources (beat desk):** Brooke Pryor (ESPN); Mark Kaboly (Pittsburgh beat); Dale Lolley (Steelers.com); Steelers.com staff; 93.7 The Fan

Pittsburgh won ten games and the AFC North in 2025. The market responded with **8.5**, the same number DraftKings posted in February, and asks bettors to lay **-120** on the Under. That is a clear statement: last year's record is not the baseline, even for a franchise that has made finishing above .500 look routine.

Kos Edge is less dramatic. The Model fair is **8.16**, only 0.34 below the market. That gap is beneath the half-win threshold before accounting for juice, uncertainty around a new staff and the range attached to a 42-year-old quarterback. The number invites a strong opinion about Pittsburgh's decline. The evidence supports caution, not a ticket. **Pass**.

## New coach, familiar quarterback, different offense

Mike Tomlin stepped down after nineteen seasons following Pittsburgh's playoff loss to Houston. Mike McCarthy is now the head coach, reuniting with Aaron Rodgers for what Rodgers has announced will be his final NFL season. The pairing offers history and clarity. It does not offer a recent sample together.

Rodgers is the unquestioned starter, with Mason Rudolph behind him and Will Howard and rookie Drew Allar filling out the room. Steelers.com reported that Rodgers did not miss a practice during the early portion of camp, even while McCarthy managed how much preseason work his veteran roster needed. Availability through August is a positive. The season question is whether that availability and efficiency survive seventeen games.

Pittsburgh gave the offense more receiving structure. DK Metcalf remains the vertical and contested-catch centerpiece, Michael Pittman Jr. adds a dependable second outside target, and Roman Wilson opened on the first line of the initial depth chart. Pat Freiermuth and Darnell Washington keep heavier personnel available, while Jaylen Warren and Rico Dowdle give McCarthy a two-back path.

The roster can support Rodgers without asking him to create every answer. The tradeoff is pace and explosive-play dependency. If the offense uses controlled throws and the run game to protect him, Pittsburgh can stay in low-possession games but may struggle to separate. If McCarthy asks Rodgers to attack more aggressively, protection becomes the central variable.

## Right tackle is the camp fact that matters

The initial offensive line listed Troy Fautanu, Mason McCormick, Zach Frazier, Spencer Anderson and Dylan Cook from left to right. First-round pick Max Iheanachor began camp behind Cook at right tackle, then missed more than two weeks with an upper-body issue believed to involve his neck or shoulder.

Iheanachor returned to a full padded practice on August 24. Local reporting from 93.7 The Fan said he completed the work without missing a drill and remained a candidate to play in the August 27 preseason finale at Buffalo. That reopens the competition. It does not settle it.

Cook entered camp with the job and had useful experience from five starts at left tackle late last season, but the right side has not consistently controlled preseason work. Broderick Jones is also returning from the neck injury that ended his 2025 season and has worked at both tackle spots. McCarthy planned to evaluate Iheanachor over the final two padded practices before deciding his game workload.

For a younger quarterback, that could be normal camp sorting. For Rodgers, it is attached directly to the season range. He can still diagnose pressure and get the ball out, but the offense cannot build its weekly floor around constant help for the right tackle without reducing what Pittman and Metcalf do downfield. A rookie winning late is not automatically a negative; a rookie rushed into the lineup after missing most of August carries its own variance.

## The defense still gives Pittsburgh a weekly shape

The Steelers remain recognizable on defense. T.J. Watt and Alex Highsmith lead the edge group, with Nick Herbig and Jack Sawyer in the rotation. Cameron Heyward anchors the front, Patrick Queen and Payton Wilson return at linebacker, and Jalen Ramsey is listed in the nickel role. Joey Porter Jr. and Jamel Dean give the secondary boundary options, with DeShon Elliott and Jaquan Brisker at safety.

That unit is why an 8.16 fair does not become an aggressive Under. Pittsburgh can win games in which the offense is merely adequate. It can shorten possessions, create pressure without excessive blitzing and give Rodgers favorable fields. Those traits travel and keep underdogs live.

The risk is burden. A defense asked to protect a narrow margin every week eventually runs into turnovers, short fields and opponent quality. Pittsburgh's 2025 ten-win season does not guarantee the same close-game conversion under a new staff. It does show why treating the Steelers as a generic aging-quarterback team misses half the roster.

Special teams also preserve the floor. Chris Boswell remains the kicker, Cameron Johnston the punter and Christian Kuntz the long snapper on the initial depth chart. In a season likely to feature one-score games, field-position competence is not a footnote.

## The schedule gets heavier after the bye

Pittsburgh opens with Atlanta at home, then travels to New England before consecutive division games against Cincinnati and Cleveland. Indianapolis and Tampa Bay follow. The Steelers then play New Orleans in Paris in Week 7, return home for Cleveland and reach the Week 9 bye.

The placement of that bye is helpful after international travel. What follows is the challenge. Pittsburgh goes to Cincinnati and Philadelphia, then hosts Denver on a short Black Friday turnaround. Houston comes next, followed by a Monday night game at Jacksonville and two late meetings with Baltimore. Five of those six opponents carry current win totals of at least 9.5.

The final two games against Carolina and Tennessee provide relief before the Week 18 trip to Baltimore, but the second-half sequence can drain an older roster before it arrives there. Rest, travel and the Friday game matter more than opponent names in isolation.

The AFC North itself is stronger at the top than last year's results suggest. Baltimore is priced at 11.5, Cincinnati has a healthy Burrow, and Cleveland has ended its quarterback rotation. Pittsburgh can remain competitive in all six games and still finish 3-3. That is enough to make nine wins difficult without making an Under automatic.

## The number is doing its job

At **8.16**, the Model places Pittsburgh almost exactly where the market does. The Under price of **-120** converts a 0.34-win difference into no edge. The aging-quarterback and first-place-schedule arguments are real, but they are already visible in a total set a win and a half below last year's result.

The Over case also stops short. Rodgers' camp availability, a deeper receiver room and a strong defense support the floor. They do not remove right-tackle uncertainty, age risk or the November-December schedule. Nine wins is plausible. Eight is plausible. The threshold sits between them because that is where it belongs.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total:** **Pass** at 8.5. Model fair of 8.16 is only 0.34 lower, and the Under already carries -120 juice.
- **Early sides:** Protection performance should matter more than Pittsburgh's historical record in one-score games. Price the current McCarthy offense, not the old Tomlin pattern.
- **Game totals:** Monitor pace, neutral-down pass rate and right-tackle help before assigning Pittsburgh a permanent low-total identity.
- **Player markets:** Iheanachor's status affects the shared passing and rushing production assumptions. Keep receiving, quarterback and team means tied to the same protection outlook.
- **Schedule checkpoint:** Reassess after the Paris game and Week 9 bye. The difficult stretch from Cincinnati through Jacksonville will reveal more than the favorable pieces of September.
