# Miami Dolphins 2026 Season Preview: Win Total 4.5

**By Taylor Brooks** · Kos Edge Analytics · AFC East desk  
**Angle:** Speed travels; durability and OL do not — basement total at 4.5  
**Sources (beat desk):** Marcel Louis-Jacques (ESPN) [@Marcel_LJ](https://x.com/Marcel_LJ); David Furones (Sun Sentinel) [@DavidFurones_](https://x.com/DavidFurones_); Joe Schad (Palm Beach Post) [@schadjoe](https://x.com/schadjoe)  
**Market (DK / RotoWire / Fox Sports, late July 2026):** **4.5** — shop juice (pick’em −110/−110 at some shops; Under juiced near −146 / Over +122 at others)

The number on the wall is **4.5**. That is a basement floor for a franchise that spent the winter tearing the roster down — and the first question for this desk is whether Miami is being priced like a full rebuild or like a brand that still owns last decade’s speed reputation.

DraftKings, RotoWire, and Fox Sports (July 29) hang the Dolphins at **4.5** — second-lowest (or tied for lowest) in the league depending on the day. Juice still matters more than vibes. The public does not love buying Dolphins hope after an offseason that stripped the roster of Tua Tagovailoa, Tyreek Hill, Jaylen Waddle, Minkah Fitzpatrick, and Bradley Chubb. New head coach Jeff Hafley and general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan have been explicit about a short rebuild. Marcel Louis-Jacques’ ESPN camp notebook frames the summer as a stark break from the flashy McDaniel years: journeyman veterans, a 13-man draft class, and a quarterback room led by Malik Willis on a three-year deal after two seasons in Green Bay.

That is the floor. The misprice, if one exists, lives in whether **4.5** already assumes Achane-plus-chaos can manufacture five wins while the trenches and durability still look like a Year-1 tear-down — or whether the Under juice is just public despair priced as certainty.

## Speed that is left

De’Von Achane is the remaining travel piece. He signed a multi-year extension and, per Louis-Jacques’ camp reporting, is available for the start of practices after missing team drills in OTAs and minicamp while recovering from offseason shoulder surgery. Hafley told local media Achane was “ready to go” as veterans reported. The Dolphins plan to run the offense through him. Willis’ legs are part of that package too — his limited Packers sample showed efficiency when the structure was clean — but Achane is the weekly volatility that can turn a three-point underdog into a cover without the rest of the roster looking “fixed.”

Speed travels. That line still holds in South Florida. What does not travel is the old assumption that Miami’s skill talent could paper over trench and durability problems. The wide-receiver room is a collection of Tutu Atwell, Jalen Tolbert, Malik Washington, rookies, and competition — not a proven WR1. Louis-Jacques and David Furones have both noted the Green Bay-style bet: win without a true alpha receiver by scheme and run game. That is a theory until September. It is not a reason to buy a win-total Over at any price without shopping the juice at **4.5**.

## Durability, OL, and the hard card

The offensive line is the quiet constraint. Miami drafted tackle Kadyn Proctor high and reshuffled the room with free-agent and draft churn after losing veterans. Camp will tell us whether the new pieces can keep Willis upright long enough for Achane’s explosiveness to matter on early downs. Joe Schad’s Palm Beach Post beat work is the right place for daily OL participation and who is winning the right-side battles — the kind of notes that never trend nationally and still decide whether Miami hangs three wins or six.

Durability is the other half of the angle. Achane’s shoulder timeline is already a reminder. Cornerbacks Darrell Baker Jr. and Storm Duck opened camp on the active/PUP list with expectations they return later. Rookie receiver Chris Bell has been on the Non-Football Injury list. None of those items alone sinks a competitive season. Stacked together with a young roster and a schedule ESPN has ranked among the league’s hardest by prior-year opponent records, they explain why five wins is still a climb for a bridge-QB offense — and why three wins is a live failure mode if the OL fails early.

December is especially unkind on paper. Division games against Buffalo and New England project as steep climbs. The Jets are the relative opportunity, and even that spot requires Willis to protect the ball.

## How a 4.5 total actually cashes

**Over 4.5** is the Achane-plus-chaos bet stretched just far enough: explosive wins against middling opponents, stolen games when Willis’ dual-threat package hits, and a Hafley defense that gets enough from Zach Sieler, Chop Robinson’s bounce-back hopes, and free-agent edge adds (Josh Uche, David Ojabo) to keep enough Sundays inside one score that Miami clears five. That path is the median rebuild path if the OL is merely average.

**Under 4.5** is the catastrophe-plus-schedule bet. Miami can be competitive for stretches, steal three or four games with speed, and still finish at three or four if the OL fails and the receiver room never separates. Willis’ career starting sample remains small. Juiced Unders on **4.5** often price that disaster as the favorite outcome.

Kos Edge research-adjusted fair currently sits nearer **~5.0–5.5** once you tax the hard card, the unfinished receiver room, and Willis’ starter sample — then credit Achane’s weekly ceiling. Against **4.5**, that is roughly a half-win to a full win on the Over side before juice. Threshold discipline: at even money (−110/−110), Over is a thin lean at best (**Pass** preferred until padded practices confirm Achane’s early-down role). At Under −146 / Over +122, the juiced Under is a **Pass** and the Over is a shop-only lean.

## What the market may be wrong about

The market may be too eager to treat Miami’s brand collapse as a free Under at any juice. Weekly sides against Miami as a home underdog can still be dangerous if Achane is right and Willis keeps drives alive with his legs. That does not automatically make **4.5** a buy on either side. The misprice risk is Over buyers assuming speed equals six-plus wins without OL confirmation, and Under buyers paying −140+ to fade a basement number that already prices the teardown.

Watch Louis-Jacques’ practice participation notes on Achane and Austin Jackson, Furones on the receiver pecking order, and Schad on whether Proctor and the interior settle before the preseason games. Those three threads decide whether **4.5** is merely a floor or a trap for juiced Unders.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total:** **Pass** as default at juiced Under prices. Prefer **Over 4.5** only at plus money or true pick’em (−110 or better) after Achane’s early-camp workload looks clean.  
- **Sides:** Prefer Miami’s speed in home one-score spots; fade as a road dog in bad weather or against elite fronts until the OL proves it.  
- **Props:** Achane rushing + receiving first; Willis pass attempts only after two preseason games clarify the early-down mix.  
- **Camp tells:** Clean Achane + Proctor at tackle → Over at pick’em is playable. OL sieve in joints → stay Pass on juiced Unders.
