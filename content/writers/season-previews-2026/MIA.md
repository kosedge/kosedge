# Miami Dolphins 2026 Season Preview

**By Taylor Brooks** · Kos Edge Analytics · AFC East desk  
**Angle:** Speed travels; durability and OL do not — and 8.5 still asks for a finished product  
**Sources (beat desk):** Marcel Louis-Jacques (ESPN) [@Marcel_LJ](https://x.com/Marcel_LJ); David Furones (Sun Sentinel) [@DavidFurones_](https://x.com/DavidFurones_); Joe Schad (Palm Beach Post) [@schadjoe](https://x.com/schadjoe)

The number on the wall is **8.5**. That is not a basement floor. It is a mid-table ask for a franchise that spent the winter tearing the roster down — and the first question for this desk is whether Miami is being priced like a competitive AFC East side or like a brand that still owns last decade’s speed reputation.

Books have shaded Unders on softer alternate numbers; at the primary **8.5**, juice still matters more than vibes. The public does not love buying Dolphins hope after an offseason that stripped the roster of Tua Tagovailoa, Tyreek Hill, Jaylen Waddle, Minkah Fitzpatrick, and Bradley Chubb. New head coach Jeff Hafley and general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan have been explicit about a short rebuild. Marcel Louis-Jacques’ ESPN camp notebook frames the summer as a stark break from the flashy McDaniel years: journeyman veterans, a 13-man draft class, and a quarterback room led by Malik Willis on a three-year deal after two seasons in Green Bay.

That is the floor. The misprice, if one exists, lives in whether **8.5** already assumes Achane-plus-chaos can manufacture nine wins while the trenches and durability still look like a Year-1 tear-down.

## Speed that is left

De’Von Achane is the remaining travel piece. He signed a multi-year extension and, per Louis-Jacques’ camp reporting, is available for the start of practices after missing team drills in OTAs and minicamp while recovering from offseason shoulder surgery. Hafley told local media Achane was “ready to go” as veterans reported. The Dolphins plan to run the offense through him. Willis’ legs are part of that package too — his limited Packers sample showed efficiency when the structure was clean — but Achane is the weekly volatility that can turn a three-point underdog into a cover without the rest of the roster looking “fixed.”

Speed travels. That line still holds in South Florida. What does not travel is the old assumption that Miami’s skill talent could paper over trench and durability problems. The wide-receiver room is a collection of Tutu Atwell, Jalen Tolbert, Malik Washington, rookies, and competition — not a proven WR1. Louis-Jacques and David Furones have both noted the Green Bay-style bet: win without a true alpha receiver by scheme and run game. That is a theory until September. It is not a reason to buy a win-total Over at **8.5**.

## Durability, OL, and the hard card

The offensive line is the quiet constraint. Miami drafted tackle Kadyn Proctor high and reshuffled the room with free-agent and draft churn after losing veterans. Camp will tell us whether the new pieces can keep Willis upright long enough for Achane’s explosiveness to matter on early downs. Joe Schad’s Palm Beach Post beat work is the right place for daily OL participation and who is winning the right-side battles — the kind of notes that never trend nationally and still decide whether Miami hangs five wins or eight.

Durability is the other half of the angle. Achane’s shoulder timeline is already a reminder. Cornerbacks Darrell Baker Jr. and Storm Duck opened camp on the active/PUP list with expectations they return later. Rookie receiver Chris Bell has been on the Non-Football Injury list. None of those items alone sinks a competitive season. Stacked together with a young roster and a schedule ESPN has ranked among the league’s hardest by prior-year opponent records, they explain why nine wins is a steep climb for a bridge-QB offense.

December is especially unkind on paper. Division games against Buffalo and New England project as steep climbs. The Jets are the relative opportunity, and even that spot requires Willis to protect the ball.

## How an 8.5 total actually cashes

**Over 8.5** is the Achane-plus-chaos bet stretched to a full season: explosive wins against middling opponents, stolen games when Willis’ dual-threat package hits, and a Hafley defense that gets enough from Zach Sieler, Chop Robinson’s bounce-back hopes, and free-agent edge adds (Josh Uche, David Ojabo) to keep enough Sundays inside one score that Miami somehow clears nine. That path exists in the distribution. It is not the median path for a tear-down.

**Under 8.5** is the schedule-plus-inexperience bet with room to spare. Miami can be competitive for stretches, steal four to six games with speed, and still finish well short of nine if the OL fails and the receiver room never separates. Willis’ career starting sample remains small. Turning a bridge-to-starter leap into weekly production against AFC East defenses is a steep ask in Year 1 of a rebuild — and **8.5** prices something closer to “respectable contender adjacent” than “painful rebuild year.”

Kos Edge research-adjusted fair currently sits nearer **5.0–6.5** once you tax the hard card, the unfinished receiver room, and Willis’ starter sample. Against **8.5**, that clears the half-win threshold on the Under side without needing a catastrophe narrative. Juice still matters: if the Under is crushed past roughly −140 without new OL/Achane confirmation, size down. Do not dress a multi-win fair gap as a lock — variance and Achane’s weekly ceiling are real — but do not pretend **8.5** is a floor number either.

## What the market may be wrong about

The market may be too eager to treat Miami’s brand and Achane’s explosiveness as an eight-to-nine-win floor. Weekly sides against Miami as a home underdog can still be dangerous if Achane is right and Willis keeps drives alive with his legs. That does not automatically make **8.5** a buy. Futures boards often conflate “team will be competitive in spots” with “team clears a mid-table total.” Those are different questions. The misprice risk is Over buyers assuming speed equals nine wins, and Under buyers treating a rebuild as a free ticket without shopping the juice.

Watch Louis-Jacques’ practice participation notes on Achane and Austin Jackson, Furones on the receiver pecking order, and Schad on whether Proctor and the interior settle before the preseason games. Those three threads decide whether **8.5** is merely high or outright punitive.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total:** Lean Under **8.5**; prefer −130 or better. If Under shortens past −145 without negative Achane/OL news, Pass rather than chase. Over only becomes interesting on a soft alternate well below the primary number after padded camp confirms Achane’s role and OL competence.
- **Sides:** Prefer spots where Miami’s speed can create one-score chaos at home; fade Miami as a road dog in bad weather or against elite fronts until the line proves it.
- **Props:** Achane rushing + receiving as the primary skill prop; Willis pass attempts only after two preseason games clarify Hafley’s early-down mix.
- **Futures:** AFC East and Super Bowl prices are lottery tickets. The win total is the only Miami futures market that belongs on a process desk this summer — and even there, juice discipline matters.
- **Camp tells:** Clean Achane workload + Proctor settling at tackle → keep Under but shrink size. OL sieve in joint practices → Under remains the only side with a story.
