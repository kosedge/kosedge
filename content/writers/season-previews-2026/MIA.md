# Miami Dolphins 2026 Season Preview

**By Taylor Brooks** · Kos Edge Analytics · AFC East desk  
**Angle:** Speed travels; durability and OL do not — desk board 8.5 is a teardown tax  
**Sources (beat desk):** Marcel Louis-Jacques (ESPN) [@Marcel_LJ](https://x.com/Marcel_LJ); David Furones (Sun Sentinel) [@DavidFurones_](https://x.com/DavidFurones_); Joe Schad (Palm Beach Post) [@schadjoe](https://x.com/schadjoe)  
**Market (Kos Edge desk board):** Win total **8.5**  
**Live shop note (late July 2026):** Consensus books have already compressed Miami to **4.5** (Under often −130 to −146; Over +110 to +122). Treat 8.5 as the package number; price the live fence before you bet.

The desk board still hangs Miami at **8.5**. The live market does not. That gap is the whole preview.

After an offseason that moved on from Tua Tagovailoa, Tyreek Hill, Jaylen Waddle, Minkah Fitzpatrick, and Bradley Chubb — and installed Jeff Hafley and general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan for an explicit short rebuild — books answered with a basement total near **4.5**. Marcel Louis-Jacques’ ESPN camp notebook frames the summer as a break from the flashy McDaniel years: journeyman veterans, a deep draft class, and a quarterback room led by Malik Willis on a multi-year deal after a thin Packers starting sample. Against that reality, an **8.5** ask is not a competitive number. It is a leftover from a brand that no longer exists on the roster sheet.

## What 8.5 is actually asking

Nine wins for a Year-1 tear-down with a bridge quarterback, an unfinished receiver room, and a hard AFC East slate is contender math on a rebuild roster. Kos Edge research-adjusted fair sits nearer **~5.0–5.5** once you tax the hard card, Willis’ starter sample, and trench uncertainty — then credit De’Von Achane’s weekly ceiling. Against the desk **8.5**, that is a multi-win Under edge before juice. Against live **4.5**, the same fair is a half-win-or-less Over lean that juice can erase.

Threshold discipline applies twice:

1. **At desk 8.5:** Lean **Under**. Do not dress it as a soft lean — the fair gap clears a full win.
2. **At live 4.5:** Default **Pass** when Under is juiced worse than about −130. Prefer Over only at true pick’em (−110) or plus money after Achane’s early-camp workload looks clean.

Never average 8.5 and 4.5 into a fake middle. Shop the fence you can actually bet.

## Speed that is left

Achane is the remaining travel piece. He signed a multi-year extension and, per Louis-Jacques’ camp reporting, was available as veterans reported after missing team drills in OTAs and minicamp while recovering from offseason shoulder surgery. Hafley has been clear the offense will run through him. Willis’ legs are part of that package — his limited Packers sample showed efficiency when structure was clean — but Achane is the weekly volatility that can turn a three-point underdog into a cover without the rest of the roster looking fixed.

Speed travels. That line still holds in South Florida. What does not travel is the old assumption that Miami’s skill talent could paper over trench and durability problems. The wide-receiver room is Tutu Atwell, Jalen Tolbert, Malik Washington, rookies, and competition — not a proven WR1. Louis-Jacques and David Furones have both noted the Green Bay-style bet: win without a true alpha receiver by scheme and run game. That is a theory until September. It is not a reason to buy an Over at **8.5**, and it is only a thin reason to buy Over at live **4.5** after pads confirm early-down Achane usage.

## Durability, OL, and the hard card

The offensive line is the quiet constraint. Miami drafted tackle Kadyn Proctor high and reshuffled the room after losing veterans. Camp will tell whether the new pieces can keep Willis upright long enough for Achane’s explosiveness to matter on early downs. Joe Schad’s Palm Beach Post beat work is the right place for daily OL participation and who is winning the right-side battles — the notes that never trend nationally and still decide whether Miami hangs four wins or seven.

Durability is the other half of the angle. Achane’s shoulder timeline is already a reminder. Cornerbacks Darrell Baker Jr. and Storm Duck opened camp on the active/PUP list with expectations they return later. Rookie receiver Chris Bell has been on the Non-Football Injury list. None of those items alone sinks a competitive season. Stacked with a young roster and a schedule ESPN has ranked among the league’s hardest by prior-year opponent records, they explain why clearing **eight** wins is a fantasy path and why clearing **five** is still a climb if the OL fails early.

December is especially unkind on paper — including trips into environments that used to be Miami’s home-field speed advantage. Division games against Buffalo and New England project as steep climbs. The Jets are the relative opportunity, and even that spot requires Willis to protect the ball.

## How each fence cashes

**Under 8.5 (desk):** The base case. A rebuild that steals four-to-six games with Achane chaos and Hafley defensive moments still lands under nine unless Willis becomes a finished starter and the OL settles by October. Fair ~5.0–5.5 makes this the process side of any lingering 8.5 board.

**Over 8.5 (desk):** Requires a leap the roster does not advertise — Willis as a top-20 efficiency starter, Achane healthy for 15+ games, and enough one-score luck to turn a tear-down into a wild-card lookalike. Pass; do not chase brand memory.

**Over 4.5 (live):** Achane-plus-chaos stretched just far enough: explosive wins against middling opponents, stolen games when Willis’ dual-threat package hits, and enough defense from Zach Sieler, Chop Robinson’s bounce-back hopes, and free-agent edge adds to keep Sundays inside one score. Median rebuild path if the OL is merely average.

**Under 4.5 (live):** Catastrophe-plus-schedule. Competitive stretches, three or four steals, and a finish at three or four if the OL fails. Often juiced; juice turns a fair disaster into a Pass.

## What the market may be wrong about

Live books may be too eager to treat Miami’s brand collapse as a free Under at any juice on **4.5**. Weekly sides against Miami as a home underdog can still be dangerous if Achane is right and Willis keeps drives alive with his legs. Futures boards often conflate “team will be bad” with “team finishes under four-and-a-half.” Those are different questions.

The opposite error is treating any **8.5** leftover as a competitive Over because “Dolphins always have speed.” Speed without trenches is not nine wins in this division.

Watch Louis-Jacques’ practice participation notes on Achane and Austin Jackson, Furones on the receiver pecking order, and Schad on whether Proctor and the interior settle before the preseason games. Those three threads decide whether live **4.5** is a floor or a trap — and they do not rescue desk **8.5**.
