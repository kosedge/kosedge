New Orleans is the one NFC South team whose model edge, quarterback plan and schedule point in the same direction. The injury report is the reason the conclusion stays soft.

Kos Edge projects **8.96 wins**, giving the Saints a **1.46-win cushion** over the market total of **7.5**. DraftKings opened this Over near even money and the August 26 board charges **-120**, so part of the case has already been purchased by the market. At -120 or better, the remaining separation supports a **soft Over**. If the price gets worse, Pass.

## Shough gives New Orleans a cleaner starting point

Tyler Shough enters his second season without the quarterback competition that shaped last summer. New Orleans committed to him after he went 5-4 as a rookie starter, completed 67.6% of his passes and helped the Saints win four of their final five games. That late run followed a 2-10 start and cannot be treated as a complete 17-game sample. None of Shough’s five victories came against a winning team.

The developmental case is still reasonable. He has a full offseason in Kellen Moore’s system, clear first-team ownership and an established primary receiver in Chris Olave. During the August 25 practice observed by Johnson, Shough completed 15 of 19 passes in shells while New Orleans worked through down-and-distance and tempo periods. Most throws were short, and the setting was controlled. The useful signal was command, not the completion percentage.

Olave briefly left that session after landing hard on a deep target. Moore said he had the wind knocked out of him and should be fine. That is a favorable update for an offense already carrying too many absences.

## The injury list is the entire counterargument

New Orleans has absorbed significant losses since camp opened. Defensive tackle Bryan Bresee tore his ACL in the first practice and will miss the season. The former first-round pick had 14.5 sacks over his first three years and was supposed to anchor the interior. Veteran Nathan Shepherd and second-year tackle Vernon Broughton are among the players taking larger roles. Broughton flashed in the August 25 session, but one unpadded practice does not replace Bresee’s workload.

The offensive injuries are more immediate. Alvin Kamara suffered a Grade 2 MCL sprain during joint work with Dallas and is expected to miss at least a month. First-round receiver Jordyn Tyson is dealing with a hamstring injury expected to extend into the regular season. Guard Cesar Ruiz has been out, while several reserve receivers, tight ends and backs have also missed work. More than 20 players were absent from the August 24 walkthrough; the August 25 list remained long.

The roster has contingency plans. Travis Etienne Jr. received most of the first-team running back work even before Kamara’s injury and can handle the lead role. Olave, Devaughn Vele and the tight ends give Shough functional targets while Tyson recovers. The defense still has Cameron Jordan, Chase Young and Carl Granderson on the edges.

Depth is not the same as health. Kamara and Tyson could miss the difficult opening trip, and Bresee is not returning. Those facts prevent a stronger recommendation despite the model margin.

## Why the Over case survives

New Orleans finished 6-11 with a minus-77 point differential, so a projection near nine wins requires a meaningful step. The strongest reason to believe in one is not the December record alone. It is the combination of a settled quarterback, a second year in Moore’s system and a schedule shaped by last year’s last-place finish.

Shough does not need to become a top-tier passer. If he sustains average efficiency, limits negative plays and converts Olave’s target volume into steady first downs, the offense can improve from 21st in total yardage. Etienne gives the backfield a proven answer while Kamara rehabs. A healthy Olave keeps the passing game from becoming entirely dependent on Tyson’s return.

The defense carries more volatility. Bresee’s loss weakens interior pressure, and veteran availability has been uneven. New Orleans can still rush from the edge, but opponents will test the replacement tackles and force the Saints to prove they can hold early downs. That is the most credible route to an Under: a defense that cannot get off the field puts too much weekly burden on Shough.

At **7.5**, the model cushion is large enough to absorb some of that downside. At **-120**, the price is barely acceptable. This is not permission to chase -135 or accept 8.5.

## Easy overall does not mean easy early

The Saints’ opponents had a .434 winning percentage in 2025, the second-lowest mark in the league. Schedule rankings based on current market win totals also place New Orleans near the easiest end. That is real support for the Over.

The sequence starts badly. New Orleans opens at Detroit on September 13 and plays at Baltimore one week later. Kamara and Tyson are most likely to be unavailable during the two games in which their depth would matter most. The Saints then return home for three straight, beginning with Las Vegas, before the schedule stiffens again in a Chicago-Cincinnati-Green Bay stretch.

Four of the final five games are inside the NFC South. New Orleans went 4-1 over a similar late divisional run last season, but this year’s case does not require repeating that finish. The South produced three 8-9 teams and no positive point differential in 2025. An average division record plus wins in the softer nondivision spots can get the Saints to eight.

That schedule also protects against an 0-2 start becoming a season verdict. Detroit and Baltimore could expose the interior defense and leave the Over ticket uncomfortable. The handicap is based on the full slate, not September optics.

## Price is the final gate

The number has held at 7.5 while the Over moved from approximately +100 at opening to -120 on the current desk board. That 20-cent tax matters. The market has recognized the late-2025 improvement and favorable schedule even as camp injuries accumulated.

At **Over 7.5 (-120)**, Kos Edge still has enough model room to issue a soft lean at low confidence. At a materially worse price, the expected value narrows while the injury risk stays the same. At 8.5, the edge becomes too thin for this roster state. The recommendation is tied to both number and price.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total:** Soft **Over 7.5** at **-120 or better**. Pass if the price moves beyond -125 or the total rises to 8.5.
- **Confidence control:** Keep conviction at 2/5 because Bresee is out for the season and Kamara and Tyson may miss the opening stretch.
- **Schedule:** Do not overreact to road games at Detroit and Baltimore; judge the ticket against the full, favorable slate.
- **Offense watch:** Shough’s pressure response matters more than clean-practice completion rates. Etienne’s lead role is established while Kamara recovers.
- **Defense watch:** Broughton and the interior rotation must show they can replace enough of Bresee’s early-down and pass-rush value.
