Seattle’s case begins with a defense capable of keeping ordinary offensive weeks competitive. Its price begins much higher. The defending champions are posted at **10.5**, and Kos Edge projects **10.81 expected wins**. A 0.31-win difference is not an edge. With the Over priced at **-115**, it is a clear **Pass**.

The temptation is to turn a stable defensive identity into an automatic regular-season floor. That skips the important part of the handicap. Seattle enters the final roster week with new injuries at receiver, safety and offensive line, plus an unsettled early-season backfield. Mike Macdonald’s structure can absorb some depth loss. An Over that needs eleven wins cannot assume it absorbs all of it without cost.

## The defense remains the starting point

Macdonald has built the Seahawks around pressure presentation, coverage flexibility and the ability to change the picture after the snap. That design reduces the burden on Sam Darnold. Seattle does not need its quarterback to win every high-leverage down if the defense can create negative plays, protect field position and shorten games.

That is the strongest reason to respect the Model’s 10.81. Defensive structure tends to travel better week to week than offense dependent on one narrow source of explosive plays. It can preserve Seattle’s chance when weather turns, the run game is inefficient or Darnold gives away a possession.

The late-camp losses test the depth behind that structure. Rookie safety Bud Clark broke his ankle in the Aug. 23 preseason game at Tennessee and is expected to miss multiple months. He had been competing for a meaningful role. Nick Emmanwori came off PUP after ankle surgery but had not yet returned to practice in Brady Henderson’s final roster projection. That leaves the big-nickel and safety rotations less settled than the championship label suggests.

Seattle has enough established defensive talent to remain good. The question for an 11-win ticket is whether the secondary can preserve all of Macdonald’s disguise and matchup options while the young depth is unavailable.

## Offensive depth changed in Tennessee

The most definitive camp loss is Jake Bobo. Seattle placed the receiver on injured reserve after a serious knee injury in the Aug. 21 joint practice with Tennessee, ending his 2026 season. Bobo was not the first target in an offense led by Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Cooper Kupp and Rashid Shaheed, but he supplied reliable depth and role flexibility.

Tory Horton had also missed more than a week of work with an injury after a strong start to camp. That does not make the receiver room weak at the top. It means an absence among the first three would now force a less proven player into a larger route share. Season win totals at 10.5 are often decided by exactly that kind of second-order depth problem.

The offensive line lost another flexible reserve when Mason Richman suffered a serious injury against Tennessee and went to injured reserve on Aug. 25. The top unit may remain intact for Week 1, but Richman’s absence reduces the combinations available if a guard or tackle misses time. For Darnold, protection continuity matters because negative plays and forced throws can erase the field-position advantage supplied by the defense.

The backfield has its own uncertainty. Zach Charbonnet is rehabbing the ACL injury he suffered late last season. Henderson’s roster projection considered it possible that Charbonnet could return before the opener, but projected him to remain on PUP and miss at least four games. Rookie Jadarian Price is positioned for a large early role, with George Holani and Emanuel Wilson part of the depth plan. Price can be productive and still carry normal rookie variance in protection, reads and workload.

No single offensive concern is disqualifying. Together, they make eleven wins a more demanding target than the championship roster label implies.

## Darnold does not need to become a different player

Seattle’s offensive goal should be continuity, not expansion for its own sake. Smith-Njigba, Kupp and Shaheed give Darnold answers at different levels. Price can supply early-down volume while Charbonnet completes his return. The defense can keep the offense out of pass-only game states.

The risk appears when those conditions fail at the same time. If the line loses continuity and the backfield cannot create efficient early downs, Darnold must operate in obvious passing situations. That increases turnover exposure and asks the thinner receiver depth to win more isolated routes. One such week is manageable. Three or four can turn an 11-win expectation into nine or ten.

The quarterback depth chart is not a controversy. Drew Lock remains the likely No. 2, with Jalen Milroe developing behind him. Specialty packages for Milroe should not be confused with a challenge to Darnold. The season projection belongs to Darnold and the starting offense until availability says otherwise.

## Why 10.81 is still a Pass

The Kos Edge fair sits 0.31 above the market. That is directional support for the Over, not enough separation to recommend it. The Over price of **-115** requires the bettor to accept additional cost while crossing the key difference between ten wins and eleven.

The Under at **-105** is not justified either. Seattle’s defensive floor, home-field environment and established offensive targets create a credible path to eleven or more. Fading that structure because of several depth injuries would overstate the effect of players who were not all projected starters.

This is threshold discipline in its simplest form. The Model and market agree that Seattle belongs around eleven wins. The board offers no meaningful discount for taking either side. Passing protects the bankroll from paying for precision the projection does not possess.

## Division and schedule pressure

Seattle opens at home against New England on Sept. 9, a useful setting for integrating the early backfield and revised depth chart. The larger issue is the six-game NFC West schedule. The Rams carry an 11.5 total, San Francisco is posted at 10.5, and Arizona’s Model fair sits above its low market number. There is no division opponent Seattle can treat as a guaranteed sweep.

The defense gives the Seahawks a chance in every style of division game. The win total still depends on converting enough close games while navigating offensive injuries. A repeat championship run does not require the same regular-season win count, and postseason strength should not be used as a shortcut to an Over.
