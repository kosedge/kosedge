Cincinnati’s win total has been a story about price, not number. Late July DraftKings / RotoWire still show **9.5** with the Over at **-140** and the Under at **+115**. The number has barely moved since winter. The Over tax has. That is the Bengals market in one sentence: everyone agrees Joe Burrow changes everything, so the books stopped moving the line and started charging rent.

Kos Edge / KEICMB (2026-07-29 preseason bundle) lands Cincinnati at **8.51 expected wins**, with playoff odds around **41%** and division title odds near **16%**. Against a juiced Over at 9.5, the model is an Under. Against a healthy-Burrow narrative and a remade defensive front, research-adjusted fair climbs toward the number. When model fair and research-adjusted fair disagree by close to a full win, Edge Threshold Discipline does not ask you to average them into a fake lean. It asks you to **Pass** — or show both — until camp and September reconcile the gap.

## The board that Burrow moves

Ben Baby’s ESPN camp hub opened with the only agenda item that matters in July and August: get through camp without losing the quarterback. Burrow was limited to eight games in 2025 after a turf-toe injury in Week 2. Cincinnati went **5-3 in his starts** and **1-8 without him** — a split the Over side quotes as a 10.6-win pace over a full season. That math is directionally right and sample-fragile. It includes a hot September and ignores how the same supporting cast looked when Joe Flacco kept the offense functional and the defense still leaked.

Camp itself has been healthier than the scar tissue suggests. Burrow told reporters his foot “feels great” and that he expects to play with protective plating in the shoe for the rest of his career — precaution, not limitation (Baby / AP / local Day 1 coverage). Zac Taylor’s workload comments, relayed through Enquirer and Yahoo camp notes from Kelsey Conway’s beat lane, stress dialogue over a rigid pitch count: enough reps to feel ready, not so many that August invents a problem. That is the right professional posture. It is also already in the price. You do not get -140 on the Over of a 6-11 team unless the market has decided the injury was the season.

Paul Dehner Jr.’s Athletic lane and Baby’s daily file both track the same urgency language Burrow used after Day 1 — intensity that has to survive Day 20, not just the first period. Chase-Turner chippy reps, Erick All Jr. back after a long knee absence, Chase Brown advocacy from Burrow — those are color notes that support an offense with its alpha pieces present. They do not create a new Over edge at -140. They confirm the Over thesis the board already sold.

## Defense spent like the problem was the problem

The Bengals’ offseason was not subtle. Cincinnati traded a top-10 pick for Dexter Lawrence, layered Jonathan Allen inside, added Boye Mafe on the edge after losing Trey Hendrickson to Baltimore, and brought Bryan Cook (and additional secondary help) onto a unit that ranked near the bottom in points allowed in 2025. Burrow’s first padded-free look at Lawrence produced the quote you expect: pocket feels different, size difference is the glaring change, get the ball out. That is real. It is also the exact story every preview has already told.

B.J. Hill opened camp on PUP after offseason surgery; the club has publicly sounded comfortable about a Week 1 target. Shemar Stewart’s early-camp knee scare (carted, scans for a hyperextension) is the kind of Day 1 noise that can age into nothing or into a depth hit — track it through Baby and Conway before you treat the edge room as settled. Contract situations around Dax Hill, DJ Turner, and Chase Brown were flagged as interesting but not disruptive to camp reporting. None of that rewrites 9.5 by itself.

Here is the Under counter the juice crowd skips: last year’s Flacco stretch showed a roster that could move the ball and still lose. If the defense’s leap is real, Burrow-plus-Chase-plus-Higgins should clear 10 wins more often than not. If the defense’s leap is “better names, same structure,” you paid -140 for a 9-win club with a good quarterback. The market’s refusal to post 10.5 is the tell. Books would rather tax the Over than admit an 11-win median.

That is the Reese Quinn read on Cincinnati: Burrow health changes the board, the market knows, and the remaining argument is whether you want to pay for consensus. We do not. Thin edges dressed as “soft Overs” are how desks lose July. Pass is the product.

Secondary markets are cleaner thinking spaces. If Burrow’s preseason snap plan looks like 2025’s ramp (parts of two games), week-to-week side confidence rises faster than futures confidence. If Hill’s Week 1 status slips or Stewart’s knee becomes a multi-week absence, the Under case improves without requiring a Burrow injury — reassess then, do not invent the lean now.

## Camp checklist before any future ticket

1. Burrow practice participation and any soft-tissue limits (Baby daily file).
2. Hill’s progression off PUP toward Week 1.
3. Stewart / edge depth after the Day 1 hyperextension scare.
4. Whether Lawrence/Allen actually change opponent early-down success in joint practices and preseason, not just quote sheets.
5. Chase Brown involvement if Burrow’s public advocacy turns into target share — prop market first, win total second.
