**Sources (beat desk):** Ben Baby (ESPN); Paul Dehner Jr. (The Athletic); Kelsey Conway (Cincinnati Enquirer); Dan Hoard (Bengals.com)

Cincinnati's win total has barely moved. The cost of agreeing with the popular story has. DraftKings opened the Bengals at **9.5** with the Over around **-115** in February. The August 26 board still shows 9.5, but the Over is now **-140**. Bettors did not push the threshold to ten; they made ten wins considerably more expensive.

The optimism is coherent. Joe Burrow is healthy, Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins keep the offense's weekly ceiling high, and Cincinnati attacked its defensive weakness with major personnel changes. The Kos Edge Model does not share that confidence. Its fair is **7.21 wins**, 2.29 below the market. That disagreement is too large to disguise: the Model points Under, the beat and roster case points toward a rebound, and the market charges premium juice for the Over. The only disciplined answer is **Pass**.

## Burrow changes the team, not the price

The 2025 split remains the center of every Bengals projection. Cincinnati went 6-11, with Burrow limited to eight games. The Bengals were 5-3 in his starts and 1-8 without him. Extending the first record across seventeen games gives the Over argument its cleanest shape, but it also treats quarterback health as the only variable.

Camp has supplied no reason to downgrade Burrow. He has practiced, handled live work and continued developing the under-center play-action package Cincinnati emphasized this offseason. The Bengals plan to hold him out until the September 13 opener against Tampa Bay after his preseason work. That is management, not a reported setback.

The supporting skill group remains the stable part of the handicap. Chase and Higgins force defenses to defend width and depth, while Chase Brown, a deeper receiver room and Erick All Jr.'s return give the offense answers beyond its two stars. Paul Dehner Jr. reported that Cincinnati's depth showed through two preseason games, with All returning to game action after two ACL surgeries and rookie center Connor Lew continuing his own controlled return.

None of this is hidden. The move from -115 to -140 is the market's way of pricing Burrow's health without moving off 9.5. At that price, the Over needs to be more than plausible. It needs to be meaningfully mispriced, and the Model says the opposite.

## The defense has better answers and the same burden of proof

Cincinnati spent as if the 2025 defense was the defining problem. It traded the No. 10 pick for Dexter Lawrence II, added Jonathan Allen inside, signed Boye Mafe after Trey Hendrickson left for Baltimore and remade the back end with Bryan Cook and Kyle Dugger. That is not a minor refresh. It is an attempt to change the line of scrimmage and the tackling around it in one offseason.

The late-camp health trend is mostly constructive. On August 24, Bengals.com reported that B.J. Hill had returned to 11-on-11 work after an ankle injury. Cook participated in position drills, while Dax Hill ran and cut on a side field. The team expects both defensive backs to be ready for the opener, although neither had completed the full return on that date.

The preseason detail worth carrying forward is tackling. Dehner noted that the rebuilt unit and its depth got Chicago ball carriers to the ground more reliably after two seasons in which missed tackles repeatedly extended drives. Exhibition execution does not prove regular-season improvement, but it addresses the correct problem.

There is still a roster-pressure point. Cincinnati released Cody Ford with the swing-tackle job unresolved. The Athletic's August 23 review described backup tackle as an open weakness after candidates allowed pressure and committed penalties against Chicago. The starting line can protect Burrow and still leave the season exposed if one tackle misses time. For a team whose projection changes sharply with quarterback availability, that depth matters.

The defense does not need to become elite. If it moves from a weekly liability to average, Burrow can carry Cincinnati into the ten-win range. The uncertainty is whether a collection of better names becomes a better unit immediately under Al Golden. The market asks bettors to pay before that answer exists.

## The schedule helps, with travel hidden inside it

Cincinnati's opponents combined for a .450 winning percentage in 2025, the third-lowest mark in the league. The Bengals open at home against Tampa Bay, then travel to Houston and Pittsburgh before hosting Jacksonville. Miami follows before a Week 6 bye. That front section offers opportunity without being automatic: Houston and Pittsburgh can stress the exact protection and defensive-cohesion questions Cincinnati is trying to solve.

The Bengals also play Atlanta in Madrid in Week 9. International travel arrives after the bye has already passed, followed by a Sunday night divisional game against Pittsburgh and a Monday night trip to Washington over the next two weeks. Rest and recovery around that block matter more than a simple opponent-win-percentage ranking suggests.

The finish has its own pressure. Kansas City appears in Week 14, and Baltimore comes to Cincinnati on a short week for the December 31 game. Division games against Cleveland, Pittsburgh and Baltimore are not interchangeable with soft-schedule averages. Cincinnati's path is easier than many AFC paths; it is not nine free wins plus Burrow.

## Two stories, neither ready for a ticket

The Model's **7.21** is an unmistakable Under signal against 9.5. The research case says a healthy Burrow, a deeper roster and a heavily upgraded defense make seven wins too low. The market goes further, asking **-140** for the Over. Those are three materially different positions.

Buying the Over means accepting premium juice before the new defense plays a regular-season snap. Buying the Under means trusting a simulation that sits more than two wins below a credible healthy-quarterback baseline. Averaging the views into 8.4 or 8.7 would create precision without resolution. Pass is not indecision here. It is the conclusion supported by the conflict.

## Betting Guide

- **Win total:** **Pass** at 9.5. The Over price has moved from roughly -115 at open to -140, while the Model remains materially below the board.
- **Early sides:** Cincinnati's offense can be trusted sooner than its defense. September prices should distinguish Burrow certainty from assumptions about immediate defensive cohesion.
- **Player markets:** Treat the under-center package and backfield distribution as information to collect, not a separate projection engine. Regular-season usage must remain tied to the shared team-production baseline.
- **Schedule:** The opponent set is favorable, but Madrid travel and a division-heavy set of high-leverage games reduce the value of raw strength-of-schedule labels.
- **Health checkpoint:** Cook and Dax Hill completing their Week 1 ramps would improve the defensive floor. Any tackle-depth loss would increase the offense's downside faster than a preseason skill-position highlight raises its ceiling.
