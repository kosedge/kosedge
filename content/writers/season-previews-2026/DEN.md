# Denver Broncos 2026 Season Preview

**Sources (beat desk):** Jeff Legwold (ESPN); Mike Klis (9NEWS); Troy Renck (Denver7); Denver Broncos communications

Denver won 14 games last season, earned the AFC’s top seed and reached the conference title game. DraftKings is asking whether it can win 10 now.

That four-win haircut is mostly justified. The Broncos went 11-2 in one-score games, a profile that should regress even when the coaching and defense remain good. They also drew a first-place schedule. Yet the correction may have gone a little too far. The Kos Edge model makes Denver **10.51**, a full 1.01 wins above the posted 9.5, and late-August reporting has reduced the most important health concern rather than amplified it.

The result is a restrained position, not a declaration: **soft Over 9.5 at -115, confidence 2/5**. Denver does not need to approach 14 wins again. It only needs to reach 10.

## Nix gave the Over case the August evidence it needed

Bo Nix’s right ankle was the central camp variable after a fracture in the divisional round and two offseason procedures. He missed most team work in the spring, and a quarterback whose movement helps Sean Payton change launch points could not be treated as fully restored on optimism alone.

The first real test arrived against Green Bay on August 21. Jeff Legwold reported that Nix played 14 snaps, completed 6 of 9 passes for 76 yards and a touchdown, and led Denver to 10 points on two possessions. More important than the box score, he had taken all of his scheduled training-camp snaps before that appearance. Nix also connected with new receiver Jaylen Waddle for a 35-yard gain and found RJ Harvey for the touchdown.

Fourteen exhibition snaps do not settle a season-long durability question. They do show that the recovery has moved from projected to functional. Nix handled game movement, operated with Davis Webb calling plays and emerged without a reported setback. That is enough to keep the model’s positive gap intact.

The surrounding injury news is manageable rather than clean. Cornerback Riley Moss missed practice with a rib issue, though Payton described it as not serious and said the team was being cautious. Harvey worked on the side during the August 25 session, while center Alex Forsyth and tackle Frank Crum were also out. Those are worth monitoring through the final preseason game and roster cutdown. None currently carries the same season-level weight as Nix’s return.

## Denver can regress and still clear the number

The mistake in either direction is to make last year’s 14-3 record the forecast. Eleven close-game wins are not a repeatable target. They are evidence that Denver played many games on a narrow margin and received the favorable side of variance unusually often.

But regression is already doing substantial work in a 9.5 total. A 10-7 finish would represent four fewer wins and still cash the Over. Denver returns the defensive structure that supplied the weekly floor, with Pat Surtain II in the secondary and Vance Joseph able to create pressure without turning every snap into a high-risk call. Waddle adds the speed and after-catch threat the offense lacked alongside Courtland Sutton. Nix does not need a dramatic Year 3 leap if the defense remains a top-end unit and the offense creates a few more efficient early-down possessions.

The first-team sequence against Green Bay offered a compact version of that theory: Waddle changed field position, Harvey finished the drive and Nix did not need to manufacture everything late in the down. It is only preseason evidence, but it aligns with the roster logic behind the model.

The counter is straightforward. If the defense moves from dominant to merely good while the one-score record normalizes, Denver can lose several games without playing badly. Webb’s move into the game-day play-calling role is also a process change. Clean communication in one exhibition is encouraging, not a guarantee that sequencing will remain sharp against division pressure packages.

## The schedule makes September uncomfortable, not the whole season

Denver faces five 2025 playoff teams in its first six games and opens at Kansas City on Monday night. Both Chiefs meetings arrive by Week 8, and three of four games from Weeks 4 through 7 are on the road. A slow start is plausible even for a 10-win team.

The shape improves after that opening block. Denver has a Week 10 bye, does not face Las Vegas until Week 11 and avoids any three-game road trip. The Broncos’ own schedule release counts 10 games against 2025 playoff teams, but several of the softer opponents arrive after the early gauntlet. That split matters: bettors should not convert a possible 2-3 or 3-3 start into proof that the preseason Over thesis failed.

The division is still the largest tax. Kansas City is priced at 10.5, the Chargers at 9.5 and even Las Vegas has enough defensive disruption to make a favorite work. Denver’s relative rest profile is better than those three rivals, however, and the Broncos close against the Chargers at home. The schedule is difficult; it is not an automatic downgrade large enough to erase a one-win model edge.

## The number and the price

DraftKings opened Denver at 9.5 and still lists **Over -115 / Under -105** through RotoWire’s August board. Unlike the Chargers’ total, the key number has not been cut since the opening market. That stability suggests the board has consistently treated Denver as a likely 9- or 10-win team despite the 14-win headline.

Kos Edge’s **10.51 expected wins** clears the market by 1.01. The mild Over juice reduces the value, and expected wins are not the same as the probability of finishing with 10 or more. Still, the gap is materially above the desk’s half-win Pass threshold. Nix’s successful return and Waddle’s immediate involvement support rather than contradict the model.

This remains a soft position because close-game regression, Nix’s full-season durability and the early schedule all widen the distribution. If the Over moves past -120 without the total improving, the price can turn this back into a Pass.

## Betting Guide

- **Season wins 9.5:** Soft Over at **-115**. The model’s 10.51 fair and Nix’s clean game return support 10 or more, but keep confidence at 2/5.
- **Price discipline:** Pass if Over 9.5 becomes meaningfully more expensive than -120. Do not pay for a 14-win repeat narrative.
- **Early sides:** Denver may offer more value as an underdog during the first-six-week gauntlet than as a favorite. Confirm Nix’s movement and the offensive-line combination each week.
- **Live reassessment:** Separate a difficult early record from underlying performance. Pressure allowed, early-down efficiency and defensive disruption will be more useful than raw wins through Week 6.
- **Division futures:** Denver at +225 is not interchangeable with the win-total Over. The Chiefs and Chargers can keep the division race compressed even if Denver reaches 10.
