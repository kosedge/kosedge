# Las Vegas Raiders 2026 Season Preview

**By Sam Ortiz** · Kos Edge Analytics · AFC West desk  
**Angle:** The model reaches six wins, but the price and Ashton Jeanty’s ankle remove the betting edge  
**Date:** August 26, 2026  
**Market fact-check:** August 26, 2026 · DraftKings via RotoWire · Editor Riley Nash  
**Model SoT:** nfl-preseason-sim-2026-20260822T013711Z (N=100000) expected_wins  
**Market (DK / RotoWire, Aug 2026 fact-check):** Win total **5.5** (-146 / +120)  
**Sources (beat desk):** Ryan McFadden (ESPN); Tashan Reed (The Athletic); Vincent Bonsignore (Las Vegas Review-Journal); Las Vegas Raiders communications

Las Vegas does not need to become a contender to cash Over 5.5. A six-win season would do it, and the Kos Edge model lands just above that line at **6.07**.

That is the appealing part. The price is not. DraftKings charges **-146** on the Over, and the model advantage is only 0.57 wins before accounting for a new late-August variable: Ashton Jeanty sprained his right ankle on August 23 and is uncertain for Week 1.

The injury is not considered long term. It still matters to a team built around outside-zone efficiency, a veteran bridge quarterback and a narrow margin for error. Thin model edge, heavy juice and new availability uncertainty produce one answer: **Pass**.

## Jeanty’s injury changes the opening, not the entire season

Jeanty went down while diving for a pass in a team period and could not put weight on the leg as trainers helped him off. Subsequent reporting from ESPN and NFL Network identified an ankle sprain and said the Raiders believed he had avoided a long-term injury. The remaining uncertainty is the timetable.

That distinction matters. Treating the injury as season-changing would overstate the available evidence. Treating it as irrelevant because the worst case was avoided would ignore how Las Vegas intends to play.

Jeanty started all 17 games as a rookie and handled 266 carries, adding 55 receptions. Klint Kubiak’s offense was already preparing him for a central role in an outside-zone structure that had reportedly fit him well during camp. Even one missed game—or a reduced early workload—moves touches toward a less established group and places more passing-down responsibility on Kirk Cousins.

Rookie Mike Washington Jr. took first-team reps after the injury and continued in that role on August 24. This is not a depth chart without an alternative. Washington had already produced explosive preseason work and earned positive reviews before Jeanty went down. He can keep the run game functional.

The gap is between functional and foundational. Jeanty creates yards after contact, handles receiving work and lets Kubiak preserve manageable downs. Washington’s emergence lowers the injury downgrade; it does not fully replace that role on short notice. The correct camp fact is therefore precise: Jeanty avoided a reported long-term injury, but Week 1 remains uncertain.

## Cousins provides a floor while Mendoza develops

The quarterback competition has become less mysterious as camp progressed. Cousins handled the first-team offense in the late-August practices, while No. 1 pick Fernando Mendoza worked with the second group. Cousins connected with Tre Tucker on one of camp’s longest throws and distributed red-zone scores to Washington and Michael Mayer in the August 24 session.

For a 5.5 total, competence matters more than upside. Cousins can identify coverage, keep Brock Bowers involved and prevent every third down from becoming an extended-play contest. He does not need to play at a Pro Bowl level to improve an offense attached to a 3-14 record.

Mendoza still expands the outcome range. A midseason transition could accelerate the franchise or create a learning stretch that costs close games. The Raiders should make that decision for development and performance, not to protect a preseason win-total thesis. Bettors laying -146 do not get the same flexibility; they need six wins regardless of which quarterback finishes the season.

The defensive floor starts with Maxx Crosby. Local camp reporting had him disrupting first-team work late in August, a more useful signal than offseason speculation. His presence can create short fields and keep Las Vegas competitive when the offense is uneven. The issue is depth across the full defense and six division games against opponents lined at 9.5 or higher.

## A better roster can still be a bad Over price

The market has not ignored the rebuild. Las Vegas opened at 5.5 and remains there, but the Over is now a substantial favorite. The book is already charging for a normal bounce from three wins.

Kos Edge’s **6.07** fair is only 0.57 above the total, close to the desk’s minimum threshold before price. At -146, the cushion is inadequate. A model mean above six does not say that six-plus wins occurs often enough to justify any price, and the 5.5 key number offers no protection against a 5-12 finish.

The Under is not attractive by default. It pays +120 and benefits from the division strength, quarterback transition risk and Jeanty uncertainty. It also asks bettors to fade a roster that added a stabilizing veteran quarterback, retained an elite edge rusher and has a late bye. The current injury reporting does not support treating Jeanty as a long-term loss.

This is exactly where a Pass earns its place. The model direction is slightly Over. The market price has already taxed that direction. The newest camp evidence applies a short-term downgrade. None is strong enough to override the other two.

## The schedule gives Las Vegas chances, but little margin

The Raiders open at home against Miami, then play consecutive road games against the Chargers and Saints before hosting Kansas City. That first month contains opportunities, especially if Cousins keeps the offense on schedule. It also contains two division games before Kubiak’s system has much regular-season evidence.

Las Vegas plays nine road games and has four sets of back-to-back road dates. The Week 13 bye is helpful late, particularly because it precedes home games against the Chargers and Broncos. The final game is at Kansas City. If the Raiders reach December near the number, three late division games will decide the ticket.

The opponent mix outside the AFC West is not uniformly severe. Miami, the Jets, Cleveland and Tennessee all carry modest market expectations. Six wins is a reasonable central outcome. Reasonable is not the same as valuable at -146.

Weather is not a material concern for the controlled home opener at Allegiant Stadium. Travel becomes more relevant in the four two-game road sequences, including Eastern starts at New England and Cleveland. Those are weekly handicapping factors, not reasons to manufacture a season Under.

## Betting Guide

- **Season wins 5.5:** Pass. Model fair is 6.07, but a 0.57-win gap does not justify **-146**, especially with Jeanty’s Week 1 status uncertain.
- **Under price:** The +120 return is not enough by itself. A confirmed multiweek Jeanty absence or a material quarterback setback would be needed to reopen the Under.
- **Week 1:** Track Jeanty’s participation, not general optimism. Washington’s first-team role matters if Jeanty is limited against Miami.
- **Early sides:** Cousins’ early-down efficiency and protection rate will show whether the veteran floor is real. Avoid pricing Mendoza into weekly markets before the rep distribution changes.
- **Late-season view:** The Week 13 bye precedes a division-heavy finish. Reassess the win distribution before using Las Vegas in December futures or derivative markets.
